An optical illusion is not proof that your vision is broken. It is proof that seeing is more active than it feels.

Your eyes send information to your brain, but your brain does not simply display it like a camera screen. It interprets the scene using context, past experience, shadow, distance, edges, motion, and a lot of fast predictions.

Most of the time, those predictions help. They let you understand a messy world quickly instead of measuring every line and checking every shadow. An illusion arranges the clues so a normally useful prediction lands in the wrong place.

That is why measuring an illusion can feel so strange. Part of you knows the lines match. Another part still reports that one looks longer. The ruler wins the argument, but your first impression may refuse to leave.

The best illusions do not make your brain look foolish. They reveal how much work your brain is doing before you even realize there was work to do.
