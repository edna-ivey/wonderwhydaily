A light on a dark coast warns that land, rocks, or a harbor entrance is nearby, but knowing which light matters. Different locations can require different courses. Mariners therefore need a way to identify a particular aid to navigation from miles offshore.

Each important light has a published characteristic: perhaps one flash every several seconds, groups of two or three, or alternating colors. A navigator measures the rhythm and compares it with a chart or official Light List. Daymarks, height, color, and radio or electronic aids add confirmation.

Historic lights produced patterns in several ways. Rotating Fresnel lens panels swept bright beams across the horizon, making a steady lamp appear to flash. Screens could interrupt the beam. Modern electric and LED systems can generate exact sequences directly.

The pattern is deliberately repetitive, not decoration. Like a name spoken in light, it turns a distant bright point into a specific place. That identity helps a crew determine position and avoid hazards.
