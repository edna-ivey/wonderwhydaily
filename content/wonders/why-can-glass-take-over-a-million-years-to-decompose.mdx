Glass lasts so long because its stable structure changes very slowly.

Most everyday glass begins with silica, a major ingredient in sand, combined with other materials and melted together. After cooling, its atoms form a tightly connected, disorderly network. That structure is durable and does not provide the organic food that decomposer organisms can digest.

Glass is not unchangeable. Water and chemicals can slowly react with its surface. Temperature changes, waves, and impacts can crack it or grind it into smaller fragments. Different kinds of glass also weather at different rates in soil, seawater, or other environments.

You may have seen claims that glass takes a million years to decompose. That kind of number is best treated as a rough way to describe extreme persistence, not a measured countdown that applies everywhere. Scientists can study ancient glass weathering, but nobody can assign every discarded bottle the same expiration date.

Glass is wonderfully reusable and recyclable because it is durable. That same durability becomes a problem when it is discarded: breaking the object is easy, but making the material truly disappear is not.
