Wrinkled fingertips look like soaked skin giving up. They are actually a sign that your nervous system is still involved.

After several minutes in water, nerves tell tiny blood vessels under the skin of your fingers to narrow. With less volume underneath, the skin pulls inward and forms ridges. That is different from the old idea that fingers wrinkle only because the outer skin swells like a sponge.

The clue is nerve damage. If certain nerves to a finger are not working, that finger may wrinkle much less in water. So the change is not just water sitting on skin. It is an active response controlled from inside the body.

Why would your body do this? One leading idea is grip. Some studies suggest wrinkled fingertips can help people handle wet objects more efficiently, a bit like channels that move water away. The evidence is still being tested, so it is safer to say wrinkles may help grip rather than that they definitely exist only for that reason.

Either way, bath fingers are stranger than they look. Your skin is not merely soggy. Your nerves are briefly reshaping the surface of your hands.
