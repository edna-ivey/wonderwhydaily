In 1955, a department-store advertisement invited children to call Santa, but the printed number sent at least one caller to the Continental Air Defense Command. Colonel Harry Shoup and his staff played along. The accidental call grew into an annual military public-outreach tradition.

Today the North American Aerospace Defense Command presents a playful account involving radar, satellites, fighter aircraft, and other tracking tools. A website and app display Santa's simulated journey. Volunteers answer phone calls and messages from families around the world.

NORAD's everyday mission really does include aerospace warning and monitoring North American airspace. The holiday program borrows that vocabulary and expertise, but its Santa locations, gift counts, and route are entertainment, not ordinary operational tracking data.

The durable part of the story is human rather than technical. A mistaken number was met with imagination, and generations of service members, civilian staff, partners, and volunteers kept the response going.
