A popcorn kernel is a tiny locked room with a secret inside.

That secret is water. Not much water, just a little moisture trapped inside the kernel with starch. When the kernel gets hot, the water turns into steam. Steam takes up more space than liquid water, so pressure starts building inside the hard outer hull.

For a while, the hull holds. This is the suspenseful part, except the kernel is the size of a pencil eraser and has no patience for suspense.

Eventually the pressure wins. The hull bursts open, and the hot softened starch inside rushes outward. As it expands, it turns into a foamy puff. Then it cools almost instantly and freezes into that familiar white, crunchy shape.

Here is the extra neat bit: the kernel mostly turns inside out. The fluffy part you eat was packed inside moments earlier.

Not every corn can do this. Popcorn needs the right kind of hard hull and starchy center. Sweet corn is delicious, but if you toss it into a pot expecting movie-night fireworks, it will mostly just sit there being corn.
