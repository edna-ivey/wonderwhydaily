Knights wore so much armor because it gave strong protection while still allowing trained fighters to move.

Medieval armor was not one single thing. It changed across centuries as weapons, metalworking, warfare, and money changed. Mail made from interlinked rings helped resist cuts. Later plate armor used shaped steel pieces to cover more of the body and deflect blows from swords, lances, arrows, and other weapons.

The important correction is that good armor was not supposed to turn a knight into a trapped statue. A fitted suit spread its weight across the body instead of hanging from one spot. Plates overlapped and moved at joints. A trained wearer could walk, run, climb, fight, and mount a horse.

That does not mean armor was effortless. It was hot, expensive, tiring, and still added a lot of weight. Research on people moving in replica armor shows that it increased the energy cost of movement. Helmets could limit vision or breathing, and no suit made a fighter invincible. Opponents aimed for gaps or used weapons designed to crush, pierce, or unbalance.

Knights accepted those costs because the alternative was worse. Armor was life-preserving technology shaped around a moving body. Its job was not to be light as clothing. Its job was to keep a fighter alive without making fighting impossible.
