The Moon does not crash because it is moving sideways so fast that, as gravity pulls it down toward Earth, Earth's curved surface keeps falling away beneath it.

Imagine throwing a ball horizontally. Gravity pulls it down, so it lands. Throw it faster and it travels farther before touching the ground. If it could move fast enough, with no air slowing it, the ground would curve away at the same rate the ball fell. The ball would keep missing Earth. That is an orbit.

The Moon's forward motion carries it along while Earth's gravity continuously bends its path inward. Without gravity, the Moon would travel away in a straighter line. Without its sideways speed, it would fall toward Earth. Together, motion and gravity create the curved path we see.

The orbit is not a perfect circle, so the Moon's distance changes. Tides also transfer energy between Earth and the Moon, causing the Moon to drift away by a few centimeters per year. Those slow changes are measured by bouncing lasers off reflectors left on the lunar surface.

Orbit is not a place where gravity disappears. It is what happens when falling and forward motion balance so neatly that the fall continues around a world instead of ending on it.
