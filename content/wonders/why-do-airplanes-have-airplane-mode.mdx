Your phone is chatty. Most of the time, that is useful.

On the ground, a phone keeps in touch with nearby cellular towers by sending and receiving radio signals. It checks in, switches towers as you move, and generally behaves like a tiny radio with a very busy social life.

In an airplane, the situation changes. The phone is high above the ground, moving fast, and may be able to detect signals from many towers across a wide area. Normal cell behavior suddenly becomes a lot less normal.

Airplane mode tells the phone to stop using its cellular transmitting functions. That reduces unnecessary radio chatter with ground networks and avoids adding extra transmissions near aircraft systems.

This is not because one forgotten phone is expected to make a modern airplane fall out of the sky. Aircraft are carefully designed and tested. Airplane mode is a simple shared rule for an unusual radio environment.

The name is a little misleading now. Many devices let you turn Wi-Fi or Bluetooth back on after airplane mode is enabled, and many airlines allow those connections when they are handled through approved onboard systems.

Airplane mode does not make your phone silent forever. It stops the phone from trying to act like a regular cell phone while the plane is in flight.
