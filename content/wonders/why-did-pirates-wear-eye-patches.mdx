The eye patch is one of the most familiar pirate details. It is also one of the slipperiest.

There probably was not one single pirate reason. Sailors and pirates lived dangerous lives, so an eye patch could cover a damaged or missing eye. That explanation is straightforward and plausible.

The famous clever explanation is that a pirate could keep one eye adapted to darkness by covering it above deck, then uncover it below deck to see better in dim spaces. That idea makes scientific sense: eyes really do take time to adjust from bright light to darkness.

The problem is evidence. Historians do not have strong proof that pirates commonly used eye patches that way. It may have happened. It may also be a modern explanation that sounds so satisfying people keep repeating it.

Later pirate imagery may have helped turn the eye patch into a standard part of the costume. Once stories, illustrations, and movies repeat an image often enough, it can start to feel older and more certain than it really is.

The honest answer is not as clean as the costume version. Eye patches could cover injuries, the night-vision trick is plausible, and the famous pirate reason is not securely documented.
