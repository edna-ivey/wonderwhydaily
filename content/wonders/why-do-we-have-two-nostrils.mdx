The divider between your nostrils creates two nasal passages, but they are not usually carrying equal amounts of air. Tissue inside one side swells slightly while tissue on the other side shrinks. After a few hours, the pattern reverses. This automatic rhythm is called the nasal cycle.

Both sides warm, humidify, and filter incoming air. Alternating congestion may give the lining on one side a chance to recover while the other handles more airflow. You often do not notice because the total amount of air remains adequate.

Separate passages may also help with smell. Odor molecules do not all travel and dissolve at the same rate. Slower airflow can favor some chemicals while faster airflow favors others, giving the brain two slightly different samples. Comparing signals from two nostrils also helps locate an odor.

A cold or allergy can exaggerate the cycle until one side feels completely blocked. Normally, though, the switching is quiet background work. Your two nostrils are not redundant holes. Together they create a flexible system for breathing and smelling.
