Our eyes can water during a yawn because the movement tightens and stretches facial muscles around the lacrimal glands, sometimes stimulating extra tears.

Yawning is not just opening the mouth. Muscles across the jaw, throat, chest, and face coordinate in a broad movement. Around the eyes, that squeeze can pull on or press near the tear-making glands located above each eye. Not everyone tears during every yawn because anatomy, dryness, and the strength of the movement vary.

Eyes already carry a thin tear film. Its oily layer slows evaporation, its watery layer moisturizes and nourishes, and its mucus layer helps the film spread across the cornea. Blinking refreshes that coating, while extra tears normally drain through tiny openings near the inner corners of the eyelids and into the nose.

A strong yawn can briefly produce tears faster than they drain, so fluid wells along the eyelid or rolls down the cheek. Tired eyes may also be dry or irritated, making additional tearing more noticeable.

These are not emotional tears, even if they look identical from across the room. They are mostly a mechanical side effect of a full-face stretch meeting the plumbing that keeps your eyes clean and smooth.
