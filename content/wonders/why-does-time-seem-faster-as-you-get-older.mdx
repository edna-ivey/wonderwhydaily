Time can seem faster as you get older because memory, attention, and routine can compress the feeling.

There probably is not one single switch in the brain that makes adulthood race. Time perception depends on attention, emotion, memory, and what you are doing while time passes.

New experiences demand attention and create many distinct details. Looking back, a period packed with firsts can feel long because memory has placed many markers inside it. Familiar routines require less attention and may leave fewer memorable landmarks, so weeks can collapse together.

Scale may matter too. For a ten-year-old, one year is a huge slice of remembered life. For a fifty-year-old, one year is a much smaller slice. That comparison may help later years feel less enormous.

Time can also play different tricks depending on when you judge it. A boring wait may crawl while it is happening but nearly vanish in memory. A trip may fly by in the moment yet feel rich and long afterward.

The clock stays steady. The story your brain builds around its minutes keeps changing.
