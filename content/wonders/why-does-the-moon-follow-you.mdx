The Moon seems to follow you because it is much farther away than the things rushing past your window.

When you move, nearby objects change position in your view very quickly. A fence post can slide from in front of you to behind you in seconds. A tree, a sign, or a house makes a big sideways shift because it is close.

The Moon is different. It is about 384,000 kilometers away. A short car ride changes your viewing angle by such a tiny amount that your eyes barely notice. With no obvious sideways shift, the Moon seems to keep pace.

That apparent shift caused by changing your viewpoint is called parallax. Nearby things show lots of parallax. Faraway things show very little. Astronomers use the same idea, on a much larger scale, to measure distances in space.

The Moon is not actually following your car. It is orbiting Earth while your brain compares it with nearby scenery. The whole trick works because distance can make a moving object look almost perfectly still.
