A firefly is not carrying a tiny lamp. Its light comes from chemistry inside its body.

The process is called bioluminescence, which means living light. In special light organs, a molecule called luciferin reacts with oxygen. An enzyme called luciferase helps the reaction happen, and the energy comes out as visible light.

Fireflies can control when the glow switches on and off. In many species, adults use flashing patterns to find mates of the same species. One firefly flashes, another answers, and the timing matters.

The glow can have other jobs too. Firefly larvae, sometimes called glowworms, can glow before they ever become flying adults. That light may warn predators that the larvae are not a pleasant snack.

What makes firefly light especially impressive is how little heat it produces. A light bulb can waste a lot of energy as warmth. A firefly makes light far more efficiently, without turning itself into a toasted insect.
