Soft snow gives way when pressure is concentrated on a small area. Snowshoe hares have unusually long hind feet covered with stiff fur. Their toes can spread, making an even broader platform beneath each step.

Spreading the hare's weight over more surface area lowers pressure on any one patch of snow. The animal sinks less deeply and spends less energy pulling its legs free. Fur adds traction and insulation while protecting skin from the frozen ground.

Powerful hind legs let the hare accelerate and change direction quickly, useful when escaping lynx, foxes, and other predators. The feet do not keep the animal perfectly on top of every snowbank, but they give it a major advantage over animals with narrower paws.

The same physics explains human snowshoes, skis, wide tractor tires, and tracks on snow vehicles. Increasing contact area can keep weight from pressing as deeply into a soft surface.

That advantage matters most when quick movement across deep powder can decide whether the hare reaches food or escapes danger.
