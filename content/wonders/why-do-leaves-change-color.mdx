Leaves change color because trees stop replacing their green pigment as autumn arrives.

During the growing season, leaves are busy making food through photosynthesis. Chlorophyll, the green pigment, helps capture light. It is useful, but it also breaks down, so a leaf has to keep making more to stay green.

As nights get longer and the season shifts, many trees begin preparing for winter. They slow down chlorophyll production and pull useful materials back out of their leaves. As the green fades, yellow and orange pigments that were already in the leaf can finally show through.

Red and purple colors are a little different. Some trees make those pigments, called anthocyanins, in autumn. Light, temperature, water, and sugars in the leaf can all affect how bright the color show becomes.

So autumn color is not simply leaves dying. It is a tree carefully shutting down a summer workplace, saving what it can, and letting other colors appear before the leaf drops.
