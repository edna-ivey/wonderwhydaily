Microwave doors have dotted metal screens because the holes are wide enough for visible light to pass through but far too small for most cooking-wavelength microwave energy to escape.

Visible light and microwaves are both electromagnetic waves, but their wavelengths are enormously different. Visible-light waves are a fraction of a micrometer long. A typical microwave oven uses waves around 12 centimeters long. The screen openings are huge compared with light yet tiny compared with the microwaves.

The conducting metal reflects the oven's microwave energy much like a solid metal wall when the holes are only a small fraction of the wavelength. Light can still travel through those same openings, allowing you to watch food without opening the door.

Other parts of the door matter too. Seals, layered construction, and safety interlocks help contain energy and stop the magnetron when the latch opens. A damaged, bent, or poorly closing door should not be used, because the complete assembly provides the protection.

The dots are not paint hiding a secret shield. They are the shield: a grid designed at one scale to look open to your eyes and at another scale to behave nearly closed to microwaves.
